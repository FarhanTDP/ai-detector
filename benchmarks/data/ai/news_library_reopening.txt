The Hartwell Public Library reopened its doors on Saturday morning after an eighteen-month renovation, drawing a crowd of several hundred residents who lined up along Maple Avenue well before the ribbon was cut.

The $14.2 million project, funded through a combination of a voter-approved bond and state grants, added roughly 9,000 square feet to the 1962 building. The expansion includes a children's wing, six study rooms that can be reserved online, and a ground-floor makerspace equipped with 3D printers, sewing machines, and a small recording booth.

"This was built by the people in this line," library director Angela Ruiz told the crowd, gesturing toward the sidewalk. "You voted for it, you waited for it, and you told us what you wanted. I hope you see yourselves in it."

During construction, the library operated out of a former furniture showroom on Fourth Street. Circulation fell by about a third over that period, according to figures presented to the city council in March, although digital lending rose sharply.

Not every part of the plan survived. An outdoor reading terrace was cut last year after bids came in almost $900,000 over budget, a decision that drew criticism at several public meetings. Council member David Okoye, who voted against removing the terrace, said on Saturday that he still hopes to see it built. "The foundation is there," he said. "It's a matter of finding the money."

For many visitors, the changes were more personal. Ellen Marsh, 71, said she had been coming to the library since the week it first opened. "I got my first card here when I was seven," she said, standing near the new fiction shelves. "It smells different now. But the big windows are in the same place, so I'll get used to it."

Twelve-year-old Mateo Diaz had a simpler review. He had spent forty minutes in the makerspace and emerged holding a small plastic dinosaur, still warm from the printer. "It's way better than the furniture store," he said.

The library will keep extended hours through the end of the month, staying open until 9 p.m. on weekdays. Regular hours resume on the first Monday of next month.
