I've been using the Arcwave Pro headphones for about three weeks now, and I have some thoughts. Short version: they're very good, but not quite the giant-killers the internet wants them to be.

Let's start with comfort, because that's where they surprised me most. I wear glasses, and most over-ear headphones start pressing on the arms after an hour or so. These don't. The ear cups are deep, the padding is soft without feeling cheap, and the clamping force is just firm enough that they stay put when I lean over to pick something up. I wore them on a six-hour train ride and honestly forgot they were on my head.

Sound quality is where things get a little more complicated. Out of the box, the bass is heavy. Not muddy, exactly, but definitely tuned for people who like their music to thump. If you mostly listen to hip-hop or electronic music, you'll probably love it. For acoustic stuff and podcasts, I found voices sounded slightly recessed until I went into the companion app and pulled the low end down a couple of notches. After that, they sounded great, with clear mids and highs that never got harsh.

The noise cancellation is solid. It handles steady sounds like engine hum and air conditioning really well. It struggles a bit more with sudden noises, like a door slamming or someone laughing nearby, but that's true of almost every pair I've tried.

Battery life has been excellent. The box claims forty hours, and I've been getting somewhere around thirty-five with noise cancellation on, which is more than enough to get me through a full work week.

Now for the downsides. The app is clunky and asked me to create an account before I could change a single setting, which is annoying. The carrying case is bulky. And the touch controls on the right ear cup are overly sensitive, so I kept pausing my music every time I adjusted the fit.

Overall, I'd give them a strong four out of five. If you can find them on sale and don't mind spending five minutes with the equalizer, they're an easy recommendation.
