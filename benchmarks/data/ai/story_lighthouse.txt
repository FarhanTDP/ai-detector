The Keeper of Saltmere Light

The lighthouse at Saltmere had not been lit in forty years, but every night at exactly nine o'clock, Elara Finch climbed its one hundred and twelve stairs anyway.

She carried the same brass lantern her grandfather had carried, its glass worn smooth by decades of salt and wind. The villagers below thought she was eccentric at best and grieving at worst. After all, the shipping lanes had moved long ago, and the great lamp at the top of the tower had been replaced by an automated beacon on a steel pole half a mile down the coast. There was no reason for anyone to keep watch anymore.

But Elara knew something the villagers did not.

On the night of her twelfth birthday, her grandfather had taken her to the top of the tower and pointed out at the dark water. "Do you see them?" he had asked. At first she saw nothing but the restless black sea. Then, slowly, faint lights began to appear beneath the waves, flickering like distant candles. "Those are the ones who never made it home," he whispered. "As long as someone keeps the light, they remember the way."

She had not believed him then. Children rarely believe the strange truths adults try to hand them. But after he passed away that winter, the lights beneath the water began to dim, one by one, and Elara understood that the promise had been passed to her.

So she climbed.

Tonight, however, something was different. As she reached the top and raised her lantern, she noticed a small boat drifting toward the rocks. It was not a ghost or a memory. It was real, its wooden hull creaking, and inside it sat a young boy, no older than ten, clutching an oar he clearly did not know how to use.

Elara's heart raced. Without hesitating, she swung the lantern in wide, steady arcs, the way her grandfather had taught her. The boy looked up. For a long moment, nothing happened. Then he began to row, clumsily at first and then with growing confidence, guided by the small circle of light swinging high above the cliffs.

By the time the boat scraped safely onto the sand, Elara was already running down the stairs. She found the boy shivering but unharmed. He told her he had been chasing the strange lights in the water.

Elara smiled and wrapped her coat around his shoulders. "They were showing you the way home," she said softly.

That night, for the first time in years, the lights beneath the waves burned brighter than ever.
