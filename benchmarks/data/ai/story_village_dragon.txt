In the small village of Brindlewood, nestled between rolling green hills and a sparkling silver river, there lived a young girl named Mira who was afraid of almost everything.

She was afraid of the dark, of thunderstorms, of the old miller's grumpy goose, and most of all, of the dragon that everyone said lived in the mountains to the north. The villagers told stories about it around the fire every winter. They said its scales were as black as midnight, its eyes glowed like burning coals, and its roar could shake the leaves from the trees.

One autumn, a terrible sickness swept through Brindlewood. Crops withered in the fields, and many villagers, including Mira's grandmother, fell ill with a fever that would not break. The village healer searched through her books and finally announced that only one thing could cure the illness: the silverleaf flower, which grew in a single place high in the northern mountains, right beside the dragon's cave.

Nobody volunteered to go. The bravest hunters and strongest farmers looked at their boots and shuffled their feet. Mira felt her heart pounding in her chest. She was the smallest and most timid person in the whole village. But when she looked at her grandmother lying pale and weak in her bed, she knew what she had to do.

The next morning, before the sun had fully risen, Mira packed a loaf of bread, a flask of water, and her grandmother's woolen shawl. Then she set off toward the mountains.

The journey was long and difficult. She climbed steep rocky paths, crossed icy streams, and slept beneath the stars, trembling at every sound in the darkness. On the third day, she finally reached the mouth of the dragon's cave. There, glowing softly in the shadows, grew a patch of shimmering silverleaf flowers.

Mira tiptoed forward as quietly as she could. Just as she reached for the first flower, a deep rumbling voice echoed through the cave.

"Who dares to come here?"

Mira froze. Out of the darkness emerged an enormous dragon, its scales black as midnight and its eyes glowing like embers. But as it stepped into the light, Mira noticed something surprising. The dragon was limping, and one of its wings hung limply at its side.

Swallowing her fear, Mira spoke in a small voice. "I'm sorry. My grandmother is sick, and these flowers are the only cure. But you're hurt too. Maybe I can help you."

The dragon blinked in astonishment. In hundreds of years, no human had ever offered to help it. Carefully, Mira wrapped her grandmother's shawl around the injured wing and shared the last of her bread.

In return, the dragon gave her an entire armful of silverleaf flowers and carried her home on its back, soaring over the hills as the sun set in brilliant shades of gold and pink.

From that day on, the people of Brindlewood never feared the dragon again. And Mira, the girl who had once been afraid of everything, learned that true courage is not the absence of fear, but the kindness to act in spite of it.
